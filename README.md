# Inverse Optimization for Industrial Decisions

Research-oriented Industrial Engineering / Operations Research benchmark for **recovering latent objective preferences from observed optimization decisions**.

## Research question

When operational decisions are observed but the planner's objective coefficients are unknown, how accurately can inverse optimization recover decision-relevant preferences and reproduce held-out decisions under new resource constraints?

## Current status

**Feature-complete research benchmark.**

The repository implements:

- a multi-product production/resource-allocation LP;
- seeded nominal and tight-capacity scenarios;
- synthetic observed decisions generated by a hidden normalized profit vector;
- a forward LP oracle using HiGHS through SciPy;
- inverse objective recovery over the probability simplex;
- challenger/constraint generation from repeated forward solves;
- slack variables for inconsistent or near-optimal observations;
- feasible noisy observations produced by convex mixing of feasible forward solutions;
- uniform and random-objective baselines;
- held-out nominal and OOD decision-regret evaluation;
- objective cosine/L1 recovery diagnostics;
- coefficient-stability bootstrap under noisy observations;
- paired bootstrap confidence intervals and exact sign tests for decision regret;
- frozen configs, tests, final report and CI across Python 3.10–3.12.

## Forward problem

For product quantities `x >= 0`, resource matrix `A`, capacity vector `b` and latent profit weights `theta`:

```text
maximize  theta^T x
subject to A x <= b
           0 <= x <= u
```

The hidden objective is normalized:

```text
theta >= 0
sum_j theta_j = 1
```

Normalization removes the positive-scale ambiguity inherent in linear inverse optimization.

## Inverse problem

For each observed decision `x_i`, inverse feasibility requires it to be at least as good as every alternative feasible decision `y` under the recovered objective, up to observation slack:

```text
theta^T x_i >= theta^T y - xi_i
xi_i >= 0
```

The solver avoids full feasible-set enumeration through challenger generation:

1. solve the inverse master;
2. solve the forward LP under the candidate objective for each observation;
3. add any violated challenger inequality;
4. repeat until no violated challenger remains.

## Noisy/near-optimal observations

The final benchmark includes a deliberately imperfect demonstration regime. A noisy observed decision is a convex combination of the hidden-objective optimum and another feasible LP solution. Because the feasible set is convex, the mixed decision remains feasible while becoming potentially suboptimal under the hidden objective.

This tests the inverse slack formulation without manufacturing infeasible demonstrations.

## Frozen evaluation

`configs/experiment.json` freezes:

- training seeds `0-29`;
- nominal held-out seeds `100-111`;
- tight-capacity OOD seeds `200-211`;
- clean observations;
- noisy near-optimal observations with mixing weight `0.12`.

Primary metric: **held-out decision regret under the true hidden objective**.

Secondary metrics:

- decision L1 distance;
- objective cosine similarity;
- objective L1 error;
- mean/max training slack;
- challenger count;
- constraint-generation iterations.

The inverse method is paired by test seed against uniform and random-objective baselines. Reports include paired 95% bootstrap intervals, win rate and exact two-sided sign-test p-values.

## Identifiability

Coefficient recovery is not treated as the sole success criterion. Different normalized objectives can induce identical or near-identical decisions on the observed feasible regions.

The noisy training observations are bootstrap-resampled and inverse recovery is repeated to report coefficient-stability intervals. Wide intervals with low decision regret are interpreted as weak identification with decision-equivalent preferences, not as a failure of predictive decision recovery.

## Scientific acceptance rules

1. objective scale ambiguity is removed explicitly;
2. observed noisy decisions remain forward-feasible;
3. challenger generation must converge or report non-convergence;
4. final nominal and OOD capacities are never used for inverse tuning;
5. held-out decision regret is the primary criterion;
6. coefficient recovery and decision recovery are reported separately;
7. bootstrap coefficient intervals are treated as stability diagnostics rather than overclaimed formal confidence intervals;
8. negative/null results are retained.

See `docs/final_report.md` for the complete methodology.

## Scope boundary

This benchmark focuses on continuous linear resource-allocation decisions with known constraints and unknown nonnegative objective weights. Mixed-integer inverse optimization, unknown constraints, bilevel learning and preference drift are separate research extensions.
