# Final Research Protocol

## Objective

The benchmark asks whether a latent linear objective can be recovered from observed resource-allocation decisions strongly enough to reproduce future decisions under unseen capacities.

Coefficient recovery is secondary to decision quality. Inverse optimization can be only partially identifiable: different normalized objective vectors may induce the same forward decision on the observed feasible regions.

## Observation regimes

Two training regimes are frozen.

1. `clean`: observations are exact optimal solutions under the hidden objective.
2. `noisy_near_optimal`: each observed decision is a convex combination of the hidden-objective optimum and a feasible alternative solution. The observation therefore remains feasible but can be suboptimal under the hidden objective.

The inverse master uses nonnegative observation slacks to accommodate inconsistent or near-optimal demonstrations.

## Evaluation regimes

- `nominal`: held-out capacities are generated from the training capacity distribution.
- `tight_capacity`: lower and more volatile resource capacities create an OOD feasible-region shift.

The final capacity seeds are evaluation-only and are never used to tune the inverse penalty, tolerance or challenger-generation settings.

## Inverse method

The objective is normalized to the probability simplex to remove positive-scale ambiguity. Challenger constraints are generated by repeatedly solving the forward LP under the current candidate objective. Separation terminates when no observed decision is beaten beyond its allowed slack and numerical tolerance.

## Metrics

Primary metric: held-out decision regret measured under the true hidden objective.

Secondary diagnostics:

- decision L1 distance;
- objective cosine similarity;
- objective L1 error;
- mean/max training slack;
- number of generated challenger inequalities;
- constraint-generation iterations.

Inverse decision regret is paired by capacity seed against uniform and random-objective baselines. Reports include paired bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, paired win rate and exact two-sided sign tests.

## Identifiability stability

The noisy training set is bootstrap-resampled and the inverse objective is recovered repeatedly. Coefficient-wise bootstrap intervals are reported as a stability diagnostic, not as formal frequentist confidence intervals for a fully specified statistical inverse model.

Wide coefficient intervals combined with low held-out decision regret indicate decision-equivalent but weakly identified preferences. This must not be misreported as precise coefficient recovery.

## Acceptance rules

A recovered objective is useful only if it produces low held-out decision regret and remains robust under the tight-capacity shift. High cosine similarity alone is insufficient. Conversely, imperfect coefficient recovery can still be operationally successful if induced decisions are near-optimal.

## Scope boundary

The forward problem is a continuous LP with known constraints and unknown nonnegative objective weights. Unknown constraints, discrete decisions, mixed-integer inverse optimization, preference drift and bilevel learning are separate follow-up studies.
